##### WHATS THIS NONSENSE? 

This is by way of being a tiny personal site hosted on GitHub, which I *think* is okay...? GitHub's a sort of collaborative coding and development platform where serious programmers do serious programming things. You may have encountered it before if, e.g., you know the first thing about coding, or you've used software that's hosted on it. Any open-source apps you might use have a decent chance of being hosted on GitHub.

Anyway you can, and many people do, use GitHub to host websites. Usually small, relatively low-traffic ones, which fits my particular bill just nicely.

##### AND YOU ARE?

Oof, gods, I dunno. Never have, really. I've been dozens of names but none of them really matter that much. This one's Coldwind. It's an oldie.

Chances are good that if you’re reading this you already know who I am. Not because I’m dreadfully famous or anything, but because someone who doesn’t know who I am would have had next to no reason to end up here.

But let’s suppose that by some baffling confluence of circumstances, you have. And you’re idly wondering, Who Hell This?

This is Coldwind: a Roman dark elf crammed into a half-century-old human junk shop. An inexplicably exhausted and perpetually infuriated trans woman, squall, shoddy fae contrivance, and cheese imperatrix.

I am a long-time AuDHD balancer with delicate hints of OCD. A pagan of dubious religiosity, I’ve a deep, lifelong interest—you could plausibly say a _[special interest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_interest_(autism))_—in ancient Rome; but only the bit where there was a Republic. Once the *pueri Caesar* showed up and started trashing the place, it was pretty much done, and my interest went with it. You can keep all that ’empire’ nonsense, and the ‘kingdom’ that went before the Republic in the first place.

My modern self (or as modern as I get as a child of the 1970s and 80s) is of Scottish parentage, but they lived in England. And I’ve never really had sufficient motivation or felt like the time was ever suitable to move north. I do love Scotland, though: I’m particularly fond of the north-east, including Aberdeen, Inverness and Orkney, and I spend a lot of time flying around those lands in my pretend aeroplane.

Some people think of themselves as having a particular affinity for a certain classical element. Some are fire people, or air people, earth people or water people. Paracelsus conjured up spirits of these elements: the *sylphs* for air, *undines* for water, *gnomes* for the earth and *salamanders* for fire. Me, I’m a *squall*: I hover somewhere between water and air. Lakes, ponds, rivers; waterfalls, oceans on the one hand; on the other the sky, and winds, and [footless halls of air](https://www.poetryfoundation.org/poems/157986/high-flight-627d3cfb1e9b7). And where they meet, clouds, and mist, and rain, and storms, and sunlight glittering and iridescing through the droplets. Give me any of those, at least to the extent that no-one’s getting hurt and nothing’s being catastrophically damaged, and I’m a happy drow.

In terms of pop culture I’ve a fondness for sci-fi and fantasy. With sci-fi I prefer the science to serve the people story, rather than the people being there to provide a viewpoint to watch demonstrations of elaborate hard science go by (looking at you, _Schild’s Ladder_). With fantasy the same applies but for ‘science’ read ‘magic’.

I’m a liker of _Star Trek_. Some people assume that means I must be a fan, but I’m not. I don’t hate _Star Trek_ nearly enough to be a _Star Trek_ fan.

I feel equally itchy about wearing the label of ‘gamer’, but I do play games. I’m mostly into flight or space simulations, building and structuring and organising games (which I am consistently very bad at); stuff that’s generally relatively peaceful. I don’t by habit play military stuff, as a rule. I used to: I was often to be found flying the Longbow Apache housebrick, the F-117 stealth housebrick, the B-17 Flying Fortress, or skippering a diesel-electric submarine in World War II.

And those games had their appeal, for sure. They involved the key elements of combat games for me: working stuff out and planning things in advance (the submarine stuff involved _actual trigonometry_), and sneaking around the place. If I’m going to do battle, like, military shit, I prefer to plan everything out in advance, and then just press ‘go’, and have everything happen quickly and quietly and be over before the enemy even knows I was there. There’s fun in that. But over time, I guess as I’ve got tireder with just, you know, all of it—_[it](https://media2.giphy.com/media/v1.Y2lkPTc5MGI3NjExOXI5MndsOGUyajg1cGtmZmp0eWQ3NDlhN3M2dTdyanp5MzFsdHB3eiZlcD12MV9pbnRlcm5hbF9naWZfYnlfaWQmY3Q9Zw/wJPou1dis1l2OENxGZ/giphy.gif)_—out there, it just feels like games like that aren’t escape-y enough any more. They end up just looking like the news.

Which is not to say I don’t still do violence in games at all. I’m not above a bit of digital shootery. Or swordery. It can be remarkably cathartic. For example, I’m currently (and have been for far too many thousand hours) right into *Cyberpunk 2077*, which does involve a deal of running around a city shooting at gangsters and hacking people’s brains. Though, obviously, I do try to be polite about it (I mean computer-hacking their brains; not axe-hacking them. Although, y’know…).

Otherwise, it’s things like _Dyson Sphere Program_, a sci-fi factory automation game; _Flight Simulator 2024_, a flight simulator (from 2024); _Diablo 4_ (running round the ruins of a continent murdering hordes of the demons that ruined it), and I’ve even lately been having another stab at _Elite Dangerous_. I’m still very, _very_ bad at it. Also *Starfield* showed up recently, and once I got used to the fact that it didn't involve any spaceship-flying at all (apart from dogfight sequences that are just like every other space combat game), I actually really got into it. It has quite the *Firefly* vibe in places.

Other interests include astronomical wossnames—I love finding out about space and planets and that… I have a particular interest in gravity, although I don’t claim to be an actual trained physicist or anything and couldn’t even begin to fathom the actual maths. But gravity and the effects it has on space-time, inasmuch as it’s possible to visualise these things, are things I love to try to visualise and to roll around in my head, and to play with in simulations where I can.

I also like taking pictures. I’d say ‘photography’ but that’s probably a bit grand for what is essentially pointing a mobile phone at something and pressing the Snap button. But when it comes to preferences there, I do like closeup shots of flowers and that, as well as landscapes and skyscapes and the like where there’s a strong contrast. (On my former site I'd have put a selection here for you to look at but I'm overcautious about storage space for now. When I find a decent hosting service that doesn't object to me linking to pics stored on it, I'll add some.) 

I also love words and language, though English is the only one I'm any real good at. But I'm fascinated by Latin and am learning it using the approach of repeatedly hitting it with a stick until it gives in. It's putting up quite the fight. And I'm relatedly fascinated by etymology, the study of how words develop. It's one of a number of subjects you couldn't have gotten me at all interested in at school but which I'm now convinced I should've been able to get a PhD in if I'd done the job right.

Speaking of, for work... Well, it's not going to be very relevant here so I'll just say I work in low-level IT. Which is to say, *low-level* as in doing basic computer admin and not *low-level* as in working with like kernels and firmware and that.

So that's me. Hope it was enlightening.
