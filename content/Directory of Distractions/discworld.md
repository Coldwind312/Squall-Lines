_'The Truth Shall Make Ye Fret'_

---

The Discworld series began with *The Colour of Magic* in 1983. Gods, I'm old. It ended with *The Shepherd's Crown* in 2015, published five months after Sir Terry's death.

The books can be read in any order—they’re individual stories and stand on their own, and their characters and situations are presented clearly enough that you won’t find them inaccessible just because you’ve not encountered them in previous books.

But they do divide up unto sub-series based on different sets of characters. These are:

<span style="color:red">**Rincewind and the Wizards**</span>—Rincewind was the main protagonist of the very first book, an inept and cowardly wizard (or ‘Wizzard’, as his floppy pointy hat has it spelt), who nevertheless manages to keep stumbling into opportunities to be heroic.

<span style="color:green">**The Witches of Lancre**</span>—Granny Weatherwax and Nanny Ogg, the most senior witches of the Ramtop Mountains, and particularly the village of Lancre (to rhyme with ‘anchor’). Witches tend to come in threes, per Shakespeare, and these two are joined throughout the books first by the young Magrat Garlick, and later by Agnes Nitt.

<span style="color:purple">**Death (and Susan)**</span>—on the Discworld, the Grim Reaper is fascinated by the humans he’s duty-bound to reap; though for all the time he’s spent working with them, he has next to no clue how they work. His granddaughter Susan—it’s a long story—is a young woman who has no truck with any of this nonsense and just wants to live a normal life where things are sensible. Her paranormal heritage is an occasional annoyance which she deals with in as orderly manner as she’s able.

<span style="color:#1d405e">**The Ankh-Morpork City Watch**</span>—Ankh-Morpork! The Big Wahoonie! The city of a million souls (many of them occupying bodies)! Such a bustling metropolis is sadly home to several people of a villainous bent. And therefore, the city needs a police force to uphold the laws, such as they are. This series follows a diverse cast of constables, led by Samuel Vimes as the head of the City Watch. Vimes is a true copper at heart, and he really doesn’t have time for the politics he keeps having done to him by the city’s aristocrats and merchants’ guilds.

<span style="color:orange">**The Post Office**</span>—Former con-artist Moist von Lipwig is executed by the city-state of Ankh-Morpork, and wakes up to discover that actually that hadn’t happened at all. Instead, the city’s ruler has decided he can use Lipwig’s skills to resurrect Ankh-Morpork’s moribund postal service. Lipwig is later ‘asked’ to turn the same skills to the city mint, and later to expanding the steam railway system.

<span style="color:#005eb8">**The Wee Free Men**</span>—a series of novels featuring novice witch Tiffany Aching and the ‘Nac Mac Feegle’, a society of tiny, massively strong, usually drunk and amiably violent ‘pictsies’. The series was pitched towards younger readers and features characters from the Witches of Lancre series in supporting roles.
